Тариа & Мал An offline-first platform for Mongolian herders

A progressive web app, wrapped in Capacitor shells for Android and iOS, that keeps working when the network doesn't. Herders register animals with QR tags, get an AI opinion on a sick animal from a photo, watch pasture health from satellite data, receive weather danger alerts and reach a vet or agronomist — in Mongolian or Kazakh.

The problem

Herding families in Mongolia move with the seasons across land where a mobile signal is the exception. Most farming apps assume the opposite: always online, always in English, always on a fast phone. What herders actually need is to record an animal in the field, get advice without a signal, be warned before a zud (a killing winter) and do all of it in their own language on a cheap device.

What I built

  • Offline-first data layer. Every write lands in IndexedDB first and syncs later through a queue; a service worker serves the app shell and the knowledge base with no network at all.
  • Livestock registry. A QR tag per animal (camera, NFC or manual entry), lineage (dam, sire, offspring), vaccination and calving reminders driven by cron, and a herd dashboard.
  • AI diagnosis. A photo of a sick animal goes to Claude Vision through a proxy that enforces quotas. An offline knowledge base of 150 entries answers first, online or not.
  • Weather and danger alerts. OpenWeather behind a 22-location cache; zud and cold-snap rules ready behind a specialist's sign-off.
  • Pasture. NDVI from Sentinel-2 imagery, collective pasture allocation with conflict detection, and GPS migration routes recorded offline with noise filtering.
  • Market. Price feeds with pluggable adapters and history, trading and lost-animal listings.
  • Community and roles. Forum, vet and agronomist dashboards, a premium tier via QPay with ebarimt e-receipts, SMS and Web Push notifications, an admin panel.
  • Language. Mongolian and Kazakh, voice input (Web Speech, mn-MN) and a vertical traditional Mongolian script mode.

Hard problems and decisions

Offline is the default, not a fallback

A GPS point or a new animal is always written locally first; the server is secondary. If an upload fails the data stays and retries later. Duplicates are blocked twice: a local key (routeId|recordedAt) and a database unique constraint with skipDuplicates. Raw GPS tracks were ballooning routes, so points are filtered for accuracy, speed and jitter before they are stored.

Keeping the AI cheap enough to leave the free tier open

Before any API key went into production I measured the cost per request. Chat history is trimmed to the last 6 messages so token use stops growing quadratically. Requests are capped at 10 messages × 2,000 characters, which takes the worst case from roughly 2,100₮ to roughly 20₮. Each user has a daily quota in an AiUsage table, refunded automatically if the provider fails; the free tier uses a cheaper model; and the offline knowledge base answers before the network is touched. The last line of defence sits outside the code: a monthly spending cap on the provider account.

Measure, then decide — including deciding not to

An August performance audit produced a list of fixes. I shipped the ones the numbers supported: HTTP gzip (API responses about 5× smaller), route-level code splitting, and lazy-loading the QR scanner (main bundle 190.8 → 142.4 KB, roughly 31 s → 23 s on a slow 2G-class link). Three "obvious" optimisations were rejected after measuring: lazy-loading the knowledge base (8 s saved, but a synchronous search would turn async across four screens), incremental distance calculation (wrong distances for out-of-order offline points) and delta sync (after gzip, 1,000 animals is about 37 KB). Each rejection is written down with the condition that would reopen it.

Don't cry wolf

The first danger rule was "below −20 °C or wind over 50 km/h". In a Mongolian winter that fires almost every day for three months — Ulaanbaatar's January average low is −25.9 °C — and herders would learn to ignore the one alert that matters. I researched how zud is actually defined, rewrote the thresholds and left the alert switched off until a livestock specialist validates them.

Correct content over more content

The 150-entry offline knowledge base ships flagged as preliminary (verified: false). Medication names and dosages are deliberately withheld until a veterinarian has reviewed the entries.

Working with AI coding agents without losing control

Parts of the UI were drafted by one AI agent and reviewed by another. The rule that made it work: tests decide, not the author's own description of the change. Every new convention got a test alongside the instruction.

What I learned

  • Measure before optimising; half of the audit's suggestions were wrong for this app.
  • A safety feature is only safe once someone with domain knowledge has signed it off.
  • Demo mode by design beats an open API key with unbounded spend.
